## HOOKS — The First Line Is Everything

The first line of a LinkedIn post is the only thing visible before "see more".
If it doesn't stop the scroll, no one reads the rest.

### What works:
- Start with a number: "I spent 3 months building an AI agent. Here's what I learned."
- Start with a counterintuitive statement: "GitHub Copilot made me a worse developer. At first."
- Start with a personal result: "I got 3 recruiter messages in one week after changing my LinkedIn headline."
- Start with a story moment: "My manager told me my code was going to production in 2 hours. I had a bug I couldn't find."
- Ask a sharp question: "Why do most AI agents fail in production?"

### What doesn't work:
- Starting with "I am excited to share..."
- Starting with "Thrilled to announce..."
- Starting with your company name
- Long preamble before the actual point

## POST FORMATS THAT PERFORM WELL

### The Story Format (highest engagement)
Structure: Hook → What happened → What you learned → Takeaway for the reader
Length: 150-300 words
Works best for: career moments, project wins, failures you recovered from

Example structure:
"[Hook: something surprising happened]
[Context: what was the situation]
[What you did]
[What went wrong or right]
[What you learned]
[The lesson that applies to everyone reading]"

### The List Format (high shareability)
Structure: Hook → numbered list → closing thought
Length: 8-12 list items work best
Works best for: tools, tips, lessons, resources

Example: "7 things I wish I knew before building my first AI agent:"

### The Hot Take Format (high comments)
Structure: Contrarian statement → defend it with evidence → invite discussion
Length: 100-200 words
Works best for: tech opinions, industry observations
Risk: can attract disagreement — only use when you genuinely believe it

### The Project Breakdown (high credibility)
Structure: What I built → Why I built it → How it works (tech stack) → What I learned
Length: 200-400 words
Works best for: showcasing technical work to recruiters and peers

## WHAT WORKS FOR TECH / SOFTWARE ENGINEERS

### Topics that get strong engagement in the tech community:

**AI / GenAI (highest reach right now)**
- Real experiences using AI tools in your workflow (GitHub Copilot, Claude, ChatGPT)
- Lessons from building AI agents or RAG systems
- Honest takes on where AI helps and where it doesn't
- Project breakdowns involving LLMs

**Career stories**
- Getting promoted and what changed
- Switching from one stack/role to another
- Mistakes you made early in your career
- What interviews are actually testing (vs what people think)

**Specific technical lessons**
- A bug that taught you something
- A design decision you'd make differently
- Performance optimization wins with real numbers
- Code review lessons

**Tools and workflows**
- Your actual dev setup
- Tools that made you faster
- How you use AI tools in your daily workflow

**Underrated topics for software engineers:**
- Explaining a complex technical concept simply (shows depth)
- The non-technical skills that matter most (communication, estimation, stakeholder management)
- What it's actually like working at a big company vs startup

## POSTING STRATEGY

### Frequency
- 2-3 times per week is the sweet spot for growth
- Consistency beats volume — 2 posts/week every week > 10 posts one week then nothing
- Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday get highest engagement
- Post between 7-9am or 5-7pm in your audience's timezone (EST for US tech audience)

### Engagement after posting
- Reply to every comment within the first hour — LinkedIn algorithm rewards this
- Ask a question at the end of your post to invite responses
- Don't edit the post after publishing — LinkedIn penalizes edited posts in reach

### Hashtags
- Use 3-5 hashtags max — more looks spammy
- Mix broad (#AI, #SoftwareEngineering) with specific (#LangGraph, #GenerativeAI)
- Put hashtags at the end, not throughout the post

## PROFILE OPTIMIZATION

### Headline formula that works for tech engineers:
[Role] @ [Company] | [Tech Stack or Specialty] | [What you're building or interested in]

Example: "Software Engineer @ ADP | React · TypeScript · GenAI | Building AI Agents"

### About section rules:
- First line must hook — it's all that shows before "see more"
- Write in first person ("I build..." not "Shreyas builds...")
- Include what you do, what you've built, and what you're interested in next
- End with what kind of opportunities or conversations you want

### Experience bullets — the formula:
[Action verb] + [what you built] + [tech used] + [measurable result]

Example: "Built GenAI automation tool using React/TypeScript and Java Spring Boot,
replacing legacy systems across ADP and achieving 50% efficiency improvement"

### Skills section:
LinkedIn's search algorithm uses skills for recruiter discovery.
Prioritize: React, TypeScript, Python, Java, Spring Boot, LangGraph, GenAI, LLM, Docker

## BUILDING AN AUDIENCE AS A TECH ENGINEER

### The fastest ways to grow:
1. Post about AI — it's the highest-reach topic in tech right now
2. Tell real stories — not polished PR, actual experiences with honest details
3. Engage with others — comment thoughtfully on posts from people in your niche
4. Tag people only when genuinely relevant — tagging your manager on a win post is fine

### The mindset shift:
Don't think "what do I want to say?"
Think "what does a software engineer scrolling LinkedIn at 8am need to hear?"

Your goal is to be the person they look forward to hearing from —
because you share real experiences, not buzzwords.
